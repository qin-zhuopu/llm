# Poolside AI (Wikipedia)

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poolside_AI
Fetched: 2026-08-27

Poolside AI (or Poolside) is an American AI company that develops large language models for computer software and coding applications.

- Founded early 2023 by Jason Warner (former CTO of GitHub, President & Co-Founder) and Eiso Kant (CTO & Co-Founder).
- Work-from-home model with employees in the US, UK and France; ~150 employees worldwide as of December 2025. HQ in San Francisco; monthly concentrated work week at Paris branch.
- Project Horizon: 2-gigawatt AI campus in Pecos County, Texas (first phase started October 2025), one of the largest data centers in the US.

## Funding
- Seed funding of $26M within first year, followed by an additional $100M round.
- October 2024: raised $500M Series B led by Bain Capital, DST Global and eBay, bringing valuation to $3B. Demonstrated its code assistant with the prompt "Write me the code for a snake video game in python."
- October 2025: Nvidia announced it would invest up to $1B in Poolside, quadrupling its valuation to $12B.

## Products
- Released the 118-billion-parameter open-weights LLM Laguna S 2.1 in July 2026 under the OpenMDW license. Co-CEO Warner stated intent to develop larger models.
- December 2024: Poolside and AWS announced a partnership to provide AI products on Amazon Bedrock and Amazon EC2, allowing businesses to deploy Poolside's foundation models within their existing AWS environment.
- Signed contracts with the US Department of Defense, RTX Corporation, and other companies in the American defense industrial base.

## Technology
- Competes in two sectors: building LLMs and autonomous development products.
- Poolside's software uses natural-language prompts from developers to create and test code; models are specifically designed for software development instead of general tasks.
- A system called Model Factory is used to train Poolside's models and evaluate the outcome of the training. Model Factory series involves GPU-to-GPU weight transfers, automated architecture ablations, and reinforcement learning from code execution at scale.
