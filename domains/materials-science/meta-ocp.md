## Open Catalyst Project

The Open Catalyst Project (OCP) is a collaborative research effort between Fundamental AI Research (FAIR) at Meta and Carnegie Mellon University's (CMU) Department of Chemical Engineering. The aim is to use AI to model and discover new catalysts for use in renewable energy storage to help in addressing climate change.

### Problem Statement

Scalable and cost-effective solutions to renewable energy storage are essential to addressing the world's rising energy needs while reducing climate change. As we increase our reliance on renewable energy sources such as wind and solar, which produce intermittent power, storage is needed to transfer power from times of peak generation to peak demand. This may require the storage of power for hours, days, or months.

One solution that offers the potential of scaling to nation-sized grids is the conversion of renewable energy to other fuels, such as hydrogen. To be widely adopted, this process requires cost-effective solutions to running chemical reactions.

### Core Challenge

An open challenge is finding low-cost catalysts to drive these reactions at high rates. Through the use of quantum mechanical simulations (density functional theory), new catalyst structures can be tested and evaluated. Unfortunately, the high computational cost of these simulations limits the number of structures that may be tested.

The use of AI or machine learning provides a method to efficiently approximate these calculations, leading to new approaches in finding effective catalysts.

### Datasets

To enable the broader research community to participate in this important project, the following open datasets have been released:

- **Open Catalyst 2020 (OC20)**: Large-scale dataset for training ML models on catalyst discovery
- **Open Catalyst 2022 (OC22)**: Extended dataset with additional calculations

Together these datasets contain:
- **1.3 million molecular relaxations**
- Results from over **260 million DFT (Density Functional Theory) calculations**

### Open Source Resources

- Baseline models and code are open-sourced on GitHub
- Public leaderboard for tracking state-of-the-art results
- Evaluation server for community submissions

### Application Examples

The project covers various catalytic reactions important for clean energy:

1. **CO2 Reduction**: Relaxation of carboxylic groups (CO*) on Zirconium atoms - important building block for CO2 reduction and fuel generation
2. **CO2 Reduction Intermediates**: Bidentate C2-based groups on Zinc and Iridium surfaces
3. **Rocket Fuel**: Dimethylhydrazine moieties on Tungsten carbide surfaces
4. **Agricultural Fertilizers (Ammonia)**: NH3 groups on Palladium atoms - critical for sustainable agriculture
5. **Fuel Cells (OER/ORR)**: Hydroxyl groups on Scandium atoms - involved in oxygen evolution and reduction reactions

### Impact

The project aims to accelerate the discovery of catalysts that could:
- Enable cost-effective hydrogen production from renewable energy
- Support nation-scale energy storage solutions
- Reduce dependence on expensive precious metal catalysts
- Drive chemical reactions needed for clean energy transitions
